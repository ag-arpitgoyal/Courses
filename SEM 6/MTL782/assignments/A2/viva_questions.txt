A. DATA-QUALITY PROBLEMS FACED
============================================================

Q: What problems did you find in the Dry Bean dataset?
A: (1) Strong feature redundancy - Area, Perimeter, ConvexArea,
   EquivDiameter are all derived from size and very correlated.
   (2) Very different scales - Area is in tens of thousands,
   Solidity is ~0.99.
   (3) Class imbalance - Bombay 4 %, Dermason 26 %.
   (4) No missing values.

Q: And in MNIST?
A: (1) High dimensionality (784 raw pixels).
   (2) Pixels not independent (neighbouring pixels are correlated).
   (3) Pixel values not Gaussian - they pile up near 0 (background)
       and 255 (ink).
   (4) Full dataset is too large to grid-search comfortably.

Q: Did you handle the imbalance in Dry Bean?
A: Yes - three things:
   - stratified train/test split,
   - scoring on f1_macro instead of accuracy,
   - including class_weight='balanced' in the grids of DT and RF.

Q: Did the redundant features hurt anything?
A: They violate Naive Bayes' independence assumption - that's why
   NB is the weakest on Dry Bean. Trees and ensembles cope fine
   because they're not affected by correlated inputs.

Q: Why didn't you do PCA / drop redundant features?
A: Trees are scale- and correlation-invariant, and KNN/ANN/NB
   worked well enough after standard scaling. Removing features
   would also remove minor information; we'd only do it for
   interpretability or if compute were a concern.

Q: Were there outliers?
A: The size features (Area, Perimeter, etc.) have long right
   tails, but the test scores are stable so we didn't need
   explicit outlier removal. Standardisation handled the scale
   issue.


============================================================
B. PREPROCESSING DECISIONS
============================================================

Q: Why standardise (StandardScaler) and not normalise (MinMaxScaler)?
A: StandardScaler centres each feature at 0 with unit variance -
   it doesn't get crushed by a few outliers, unlike MinMax which
   is highly outlier-sensitive.

Q: Why not scale tree models?
A: Trees split on thresholds - only the *order* of values matters,
   not their magnitude. Scaling is a no-op for them.

Q: Why fit the scaler only on the training set?
A: Otherwise you leak test-set statistics into preprocessing,
   which biases your evaluation upward.

Q: Why a *new* StandardScaler for the full MNIST?
A: The subset scaler was fit on 5 600 images - its mean/std isn't
   representative of the 60 000-image training set. Each training
   set should fit its own scaler.

Q: Why label-encode the Dry Bean target for PyTorch but not for sklearn?
A: sklearn classifiers handle string labels natively. PyTorch's
   CrossEntropyLoss expects integer class indices, so we used
   LabelEncoder.


============================================================
C. TRAIN / TEST METHODOLOGY
============================================================

Q: Why stratified split?
A: Keeps the class proportions in train and test the same as in
   the full data - essential for imbalanced Q1, prevents flukes
   for the small Q2 subset.

Q: Why an 80/20 split?
A: Standard rule of thumb that gives enough test samples for
   stable accuracy estimates while keeping most of the data for
   training.

Q: Why 5-fold CV in Q1 and only 3-fold in Q2?
A: Fewer folds in Q2 because grid search runs 5 classifiers x
   many configurations; 3-fold cuts compute by ~40 % with similar
   reliability. Q1's data is small enough that 5-fold is cheap.

Q: Why a *separate inner* validation split for the PyTorch ANN tuning?
A: PyTorch isn't a sklearn estimator so we can't use cross_val_score.
   A held-out 20 % validation slice is the simplest substitute.

Q: What does random_state=42 do?
A: Fixes the pseudo-random shuffling so the same split, same model
   initialisation, and same CV folds happen every run - makes
   results reproducible.


============================================================
D. WHY EACH CLASSIFIER BEHAVES THE WAY IT DOES
============================================================

Q: Why is Naive Bayes so weak on MNIST?
A: Two assumption violations:
   (1) pixels aren't conditionally independent given the digit
       class (neighbouring pixels are highly correlated);
   (2) pixel intensities aren't Gaussian.
   So NB picks the wrong likelihood and miscalibrates badly.

Q: Why does Naive Bayes get *worse* with more data?
A: More samples just sharpen the wrong distribution. Its bias is
   too high - additional data can't fix a structural model error.
   Textbook "high bias, low variance" failure mode.

Q: Why is a single Decision Tree weak on MNIST but Random Forest strong?
A: A single tree is unstable and high-variance on 784-D data -
   every split is one of many similar pixels. RF averages many
   decorrelated trees, dropping variance dramatically (bias-
   variance trade-off).

Q: Why does KNN need scaled features?
A: KNN uses Euclidean / Manhattan distances. An unscaled feature
   with a much larger range (Area in pixels) dominates the distance,
   drowning out everything else.

Q: Why is KNN slow on full MNIST?
A: Training is O(1) (it just stores points), but PREDICTION is
   O(n_train * n_test * d). For 60 000 * 10 000 * 784 that's
   billions of operations - that's why Q2 took 715 s.

Q: Why is ANN/MLP best on MNIST?
A: Neural nets can learn arbitrary non-linear feature combinations.
   With enough hidden units, an MLP can approximate the digit-
   classification boundary far better than a Gaussian-shape model
   or a simple distance-based classifier.


============================================================
E. TUNING AND METRICS
============================================================

Q: Difference between Grid and Randomized search?
A: Grid evaluates every combination of a discrete grid (exhaustive).
   Randomized samples n_iter configurations from given distributions
   (cheap, scales to continuous parameters).

Q: When does Randomized beat Grid?
A: When the search space has continuous parameters or is so large
   that an exhaustive grid is unfeasible. With n_iter=15 on
   continuous alpha and lr_init we covered a far wider region than
   a comparable grid.

Q: When does Grid win?
A: Tiny discrete spaces - like KNN's (k, p, weights) triplet -
   where every combination matters and 28 fits is cheap.

Q: Why F1-macro and not F1-weighted?
A: Macro treats every class equally - it's the right choice for
   imbalanced data because under-represented classes count fully.
   Weighted averages by support, so the majority class still
   dominates.

Q: What is log-loss?
A: The negative log-likelihood of the true labels under the
   predicted probabilities. Penalises confident wrong predictions
   strongly. Sensitive to CALIBRATION, not just rank.

Q: What does ROC-AUC measure in multi-class?
A: We use one-vs-rest: build 10 binary problems (digit k vs the
   rest), compute AUC for each, average. AUC is threshold-free -
   it measures how well the model RANKS class-k examples above
   non-k examples.

Q: Why is ANN's log-loss best on the subset even though RF has
   higher accuracy?
A: RF outputs are averaged tree votes - they tend to be over-
   confident or jumpy. ANN is trained directly with cross-entropy
   loss, so its softmax probabilities are better calibrated.
   Accuracy and log-loss measure different things.

Q: Why didn't the tuned models beat the defaults by much?
A: RF, KNN with sensible defaults are already close to their
   ceiling on a relatively easy dataset. Tuning shaved
   ~0.2-2.5 %. Big gains usually come from feature engineering
   or model class, not hyperparameters.


============================================================
F. CROSS-VALIDATION SPECIFICS
============================================================

Q: What is k-fold CV?
A: Split training data into k equal folds. For each fold, train
   on the other k-1 and score on the held-out fold. Average the
   k scores -> robust performance estimate.

Q: Why stratified k-fold?
A: Each fold preserves class proportions - same reason as
   stratified split, more reliable on imbalanced data.

Q: Why CV + a separate test set?
A: CV chooses the best hyperparameters on the training set; the
   held-out test set gives one final unbiased number. If you tune
   on the test set, you leak it.

Q: What is var_smoothing in GaussianNB?
A: A small constant added to the variance of every feature to
   avoid division by zero / numerical instability when a feature
   has near-zero variance in some class.


============================================================
G. THINGS SPECIFIC TO OUR DESIGN
============================================================

Q: Why subset MNIST to 7 000 first and then retrain on full?
A: To make grid + random search finish in minutes on Kaggle.
   Hyperparameters generalise from a stratified subset, so the
   cheap tuning gave near-optimal configs that we then refit on
   the full 60k.

Q: Did the subset hyperparameters transfer well?
A: Yes - every model gained accuracy when retrained on the full
   60k (DT +9.3 %, KNN +3.2 %, RF +2.1 %, ANN +3.8 %). Only NB
   regressed, but that's a model issue, not a tuning issue.

Q: Why bump MLP's max_iter from 30 to 200?
A: At 30 iterations on the subset the loss hadn't converged
   (sklearn raised ConvergenceWarning). On 60k samples it would
   be even further from the optimum, so we gave it more iterations.

Q: Why use PyTorch instead of MLPClassifier?
A: Two reasons:
   (1) GPU support - PyTorch trains the ANN on the T4 in ~1
       minute vs several minutes on CPU.
   (2) Full control over training loop (custom dropout, batch
       size, optimizer).

Q: Why batch size 128 in Q1 and 256 in Q2 final?
A: Q2 final has 60k samples - bigger batches give better GPU
   utilisation and faster epochs. Q1's 10k can fit smaller batches
   without slowdown.

Q: Are 40 epochs enough for the ANN?
A: Plenty for these dataset sizes - both training and validation
   loss flatten well before epoch 40. More would risk overfitting
   unless we add explicit regularisation.


============================================================
H. SANITY-CHECK QUESTIONS
============================================================

Q: What if you had imbalanced MNIST?
A: Use f1_macro, class_weight='balanced', oversampling of minority
   classes (e.g. SMOTE), or focal loss for the ANN.

Q: What if features had missing values?
A: Numerical: median or mean imputation, possibly with an
   indicator feature.
   Categorical: most-frequent or "missing" category.
   Or use a model that handles them natively (HistGradientBoosting).

Q: How would you scale this to the full 70k MNIST + bigger model?
A: Switch to a CNN; it exploits spatial structure (which our flat
   MLP throws away). Plus mini-batch SGD on GPU.

Q: What's the bias-variance trade-off in your results?
A: Naive Bayes      -> high bias (over-simplified, underfits).
   Single DT MNIST  -> high variance (different trains give
                       very different trees).
   Random Forest    -> reduces variance by averaging.
   Big ANN          -> low bias, controlled variance via
                       weight-decay + dropout.

Q: What does n_jobs=-1 do?
A: Tells sklearn to use all available CPU cores in parallel for
   fit / predict.
